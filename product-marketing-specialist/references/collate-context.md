## Current working definition

Collate is the AI for Data platform and the company behind OpenMetadata, described in current messaging as the open context layer for AI. The platform combines a governed data foundation—discovery, cataloging, lineage, quality, observability, governance, contracts, and data products—with AI-native workflows and agentic automation.

The architecture frame is **Connect. Reason. Remember.**:

- **Context / Connect:** connect data, metadata, lineage, and operational signals.
- **Ontology / Reason:** model business meaning, relationships, metrics, and governed semantics.
- **Organizational memory / Remember:** retain decisions, context, and feedback so people and agents do not repeatedly reconstruct the same understanding.

Use “AI for Data” and “open context layer for AI” as current working language. Do not reintroduce retired product sub-brands such as AskCollate or Collate AI as primary product names unless the task explicitly concerns historical material or the source of truth has changed.

## Core problem and value logic

Organizations have invested in data platforms, but teams still spend substantial effort validating, reconciling, documenting, and fixing data. Definitions and metadata are fragmented, so people cannot reliably find or interpret data. As AI consumes enterprise data, missing context and governed meaning increase the risk of wrong answers, weak analytics, and compliance problems at machine speed.

The strategic value logic is:

1. Collate connects fragmented metadata and operational signals.
2. It models business meaning through ontology, glossary, lineage, and governed metrics.
3. It preserves organizational memory and makes context available to people and agents.
4. This enables trusted discovery and analytics, governed AI, and more automated data-management work.

Do not collapse this into generic “data management” language when the task is about AI. Do not collapse it into an AI claim when the buyer is entering through a proven data-foundation use case.

## Current message pillars

### 1. Open context layer

Collate is built on OpenMetadata and an open foundation. The intended value is portability, shared context, and a data model that is not locked to one proprietary cloud or LLM vendor. Current working proof includes the OpenMetadata community and deployment footprint, Apache 2.0 foundation, and open standards. Verify counts before citing them.

### 2. Agentic orchestration grounded in business meaning

Collate's AI-native workflows and agents operate over governed context, ontology, lineage, permissions, and auditability rather than treating raw retrieval or model choice as the whole solution. Relevant capabilities include documentation, tiering, classification, quality automation, AI Analytics, AI Studio, and SDK/MCP interfaces. Verify availability and deployment scope before making a specific claim.

### 3. One context layer across clouds and LLMs

The intended differentiation is a governed ontology and organizational memory that can serve multiple clouds, data platforms, and AI consumers. The buyer benefit is optionality and consistent meaning rather than dependence on one cloud catalog or model runtime. Prove this with current deployment, connector, SDK, MCP, and governance documentation.

## Evidence and claims discipline

Approved-looking proof in the current framework includes customer examples from Mango, Woop, NW, Gorgias, Loggi, FREENOW, Thndr, inDrive, Carrefour Brazil, and others. Use a quote or metric only when the exact source and approval status are known. A source file may contain a placeholder or internal-only proof point; do not turn it into external copy automatically.

The current framework includes a text-to-SQL benchmark claim involving an increase to 76.5% on a named benchmark. Treat this as a high-sensitivity claim: verify the benchmark, model versions, comparison, scope, date, and approval before use.

Do not lead with raw connector counts without checking the current methodology. Do not use retired or explicitly unsupported claims such as “Metadata Lake,” “bidirectional GitHub sync,” or “18x faster on Redshift.”

## Competitive framing

The most durable competitive framing is not “competitor lacks feature X.” Compare the buyer's alternatives and the strength of the promise:

- Legacy or proprietary catalogs versus an open context foundation
- Point solutions versus one architecture spanning discovery, quality, lineage, observability, governance, and AI workflows
- Raw-schema or retrieval-based AI versus AI grounded in ontology, permissions, lineage, and organizational memory
- Manual stewardship versus ongoing automation and agentic workflows
- Cloud or model lock-in versus deployment and LLM optionality

Competitive claims are perishable. Re-check the current comparison file before using a specific statement about DataHub, Alation, Atlan, Collibra, Unity Catalog, Snowflake Horizon, or another vendor.

## Messaging guardrails

- Lead with the buyer's job and trigger, not internal architecture.
- Explain “context layer,” “ontology,” and “memory” in buyer language when first introduced.
- Avoid implying that AI alone fixes poor data foundations.
- Avoid claiming Collate is uniquely capable unless the evidence supports uniqueness against relevant alternatives.
- Keep “OpenMetadata” and “Collate” roles clear: OpenMetadata is the open foundation; Collate is the commercial AI for Data platform built on it.
- Preserve the distinction between current product truth, roadmap, beta, internal narrative, and hypothesis.

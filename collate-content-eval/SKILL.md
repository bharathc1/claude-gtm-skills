# Collate Content Eval

Use this skill as both a drafting workflow and a publication gate.

## Load the standard

Read `references/suresh-content-eval.md` completely before drafting, rewriting, or evaluating content. Treat it as authoritative for the evaluation criteria, hard gates, scoring anchors, and required output.

## Establish context

Before writing or scoring, determine:

- Asset type and intended length.
- Target reader and role.
- Reader relationship: OpenMetadata user, Collate customer, prospect, or other.
- Initiative state: AI absent, planned, in production, or mixed.
- Recognizable problem the content must open with.
- Intended action and primary CTA.
- Current messaging, product-status, and evidence sources available in the workspace or supplied by the user.

Infer these only when the surrounding material supports a low-risk conclusion. Otherwise, identify the missing decision. Never manufacture an audience insight, proof point, product capability, or availability claim to complete the draft.

## Create or evaluate content

For new content:

1. Form one connected argument from problem to action.
2. Draft in plain customer language.
3. Explain a Collate product name at first mention when it is necessary.
4. Include only capabilities that resolve the stated problem.
5. Use one primary action unless the format explicitly requires another hierarchy.

For existing content, preserve the intended meaning and supported facts while correcting failures against the standard.

## Run the publication gate

After every draft or revision:

1. Run all hard gates.
2. Score all eight dimensions independently.
3. Quote evidence from the draft for each score.
4. Identify exactly what prevents any dimension from reaching 10.
5. Revise every correctable issue automatically.
6. Re-run the hard gates and re-score from scratch.
7. Continue until all eight scores are 10 or an evidence, availability, audience, or stakeholder blocker remains.

Do not average scores. Do not inflate a score because the overall draft is strong. Do not label content `READY` unless every hard gate passes and the score vector is `10 · 10 · 10 · 10 · 10 · 10 · 10 · 10`.

## Handle blockers

When a perfect score depends on missing information:

- Produce the strongest safe version possible.
- Mark the status `BLOCKED FROM 10/10`, not `READY`.
- Name the exact unsupported language or unresolved choice.
- State the source, calculation, product-status confirmation, or stakeholder decision required.
- Do not weaken factual standards merely to achieve a perfect score.

## Return results

When the user asks for content creation or rewriting, lead with the best final copy. Follow it with:

- Hard-gate result.
- Eight-part score vector.
- Publication status.
- Any unresolved blockers.

Keep the handoff proportional to the asset. Use the complete scorecard from the reference when the user asks for a formal evaluation, audit, or detailed rationale.
